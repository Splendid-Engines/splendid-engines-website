The Best Outbound Isn't Personalized. It's Personal.

Personalizing outbound is the rage. We think that is backwards.

Evan F.P.
Co-Founder, Splendid Engines · June 28, 2026
The premise

Your inbox got more depressing this year, and you are not the only one feeling it.

Doctors, generals, and executives are buried in the same thing: outbound that a machine “personalized” and no human stood behind.

Personalizing outbound is the rage. Everyone is racing to do more of it, faster, with AI. We think that is backwards.

The best outbound isn’t personalized. It’s personal. Personalized just means a machine filled in a variable. Personal means a person spent real time on you. Your reader knows the difference, and reads it in seconds. We call that signal digital body language.

Digital body language: the often-unquantifiable semiotic payload attached to any unit of digital content or communication, derived from the content itself, all of its variables, and the environment in which it is consumed.

In plainer terms: everything a message signals beyond its words. The cues a reader uses, mostly without thinking, to sense how much real human attention went into it. We read it in person without trying. We read it online too.

The difference
Personalized
  • A machine filled in a variable
  • Free, infinite, to thousands
  • Anyone can fake it
  • Reads as: processed
Personal
  • A person spent real time on you
  • Costly, finite, to one
  • No one can fake it
  • Reads as: seen
Felt first, in seconds  →  measured later, downstream (replies, meetings, pipeline)
The feeling leads. The metric lags. Both are your digital body language.

Marketers read it instinctively. They also know the hard part: explaining it to a C-suite that, rightly, wants P&L, funnel conversion, and customer acquisition cost. That gap is not a problem to fix. It is a productive tension, and it belongs there.

You feel the difference before you can measure it. The numbers, replies, meetings booked, pipeline, come downstream.

A lens from game design

I came to this from game design, where there is a framework called MDA: mechanics, dynamics, aesthetics.

Mechanics are the rules and the pieces, the part the designer builds. Dynamics are the patterns of behavior that emerge once people actually play. Camping in a shooter is a dynamic: no one designed it, it just appears. Aesthetics are what the player feels.

The designer works from the mechanics up. The player learns the mechanics, plays them out, and feels the aesthetic. The two sides start at opposite ends and meet in the middle, at the dynamics.

Designer builds →
Mechanics → Dynamics → Aesthetics
Aesthetics ← Dynamics ← Mechanics
← Reader feels
They meet in the middle, at the dynamics.

Communication is the same game. The mechanics are the words and formats you can send, and AI just made those nearly free and nearly infinite. But the dynamics, how people behave, and the aesthetics, what they feel, are still human.

The mechanics changed. The game did not. It is still one person trying to reach another.

That is why time is the tell. Michael Spence won a Nobel for the idea underneath it:

The tell

A signal is believed only when it is hard to fake for the people who do not mean it.

A black belt works as a signal because it is expensive to earn. Human time works the same way. The people who do not care will not spend it.

There is a second lens, about what is intrinsically human. The writing teacher Janet Burroway has a line: in literature, only trouble is interesting. She goes on to explain that a landscape can be beautiful, but it will never be tragic, and it will never be funny.

Those need a person. Nothing carries dramatic or narrative energy without human involvement. Meaning does not live in the scenery. It lives in the connection between people.

AI has made it easier than ever to feed the machines that harvest human attention in vast quantities. The result is a flood of cheap content and a reader who has learned to discount all of it.

So work from a safe assumption: no one is fooled by your digital communication. If that is not true yet, it will be soon.

What to do instead

The opportunity, as usual, asks for the courage to break from the herd. Seek out the communication that betrays real human time: effort, attention, reflection, a decision about this specific person.

There are companies that will fake that for you. One sells machine-written notes in imitation handwriting. It is mind-boggling, a little hilarious, and a lie with a timer on it. The whole value was that a person showed up. Outsource that, and the only thing left to discover is the lie.

So spend the time. Block an hour a week for human communication, the way you block a board meeting, and protect it.

Try it now

Reconnect with one person.

Drop this into your AI assistant:

Help me reconnect with one person, for real, not at scale.
1. Look at my calendar next week, find a free hour, block it, title it "Human communication time."
2. Help me choose the person: someone I genuinely hit it off with but have not spoken to in a long time. If my CRM or contacts are connected, surface a few dormant contacts I had real rapport with and let me pick. If not, ask me two or three questions to jog my memory.
3. Pick the format that fits how I can reach them: a phone number means a call or a voice note, a mailing address means a handwritten note, an email means a short personal message. Choose the one that costs me a little and reads as human.
4. Draft it in my voice. Short, specific to them, no pitch.
5. Tell me the exact next step to send it.

Needs calendar access. Optional: CRM or contacts. No connections? It runs on your answers. Works with any current assistant.

Formats sort themselves by how hard they are to fake.

Hardest to fake Presumed AI
Handwritten note Real letter Live call Voice memo Personal email Mass email Generic DM Auto-post

AI can flood the cheap end. It cannot counterfeit the expensive end, because the expense is the point.

The strongest case against me is simple. If the reader cannot tell, who cares how it was made? Output is output.

But flood a channel with free words and the words stop working. The one who still spends real time is the one who gets through. You do not win by generating more. You win by being the rare signal that costs something and shows consideration.

I say all this as the co-founder of an AI-native company. Like everyone else, we have the tools for economies of scale, and we use them when it makes sense. We also build humanity into the same engine: real voice profiles, human-level cultural signals, leveraged humanity.

We are not betting on AI instead of people, or people instead of AI. We are betting the winner is the combination, because people follow people.

One move we like: let AI find the people who would get the most from your human time, then spend it there. Keep your efficiency plays. Just stop treating efficiency as the whole game.

This article is the result of no less than three hours of human time, effort, and attention, with AI tools augmenting it.

It is better than what I would have made alone. It is still me. I went in and backspaced individual words and swapped them by hand.

My hand is in the work. Make sure yours is in yours.
