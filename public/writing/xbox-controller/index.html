How I’m Getting 10x More Done With an Xbox Controller, Wireless Headphones, and Claude

The exact rig, and how to build your own.

By Evan F.P.

I’m writing this on a wireless headset, walking laps around my office, with an Xbox controller in my hands. I haven’t touched my keyboard in an hour.

A car-guy friend taught me why this works. I told him I wanted a cold air intake for my GTI because it sounds fun. “You’ll need a performance exhaust too,” he said. “You’ll be pulling in more air. It needs somewhere to go.” Speed up one part of a system and you find the next slow part.

For most of my life, my exhaust was a keyboard. My brain runs hot and wide, and my hands could never keep up. A wireless headset, an Xbox controller, and Claude fixed that. I’m getting about ten times more done than I did a year ago.

Here is the whole rig and how to build it. It took me an afternoon to set up and about a week to feel fast.

Who this is for

If your brain runs ahead of your fingers, if you can’t sit still, if you’ve watched the AI wave and wondered whether it was built for someone else: this is for you.

If you love a quiet desk and a keyboard, it probably isn’t. That’s fine. Skip it.

One disclaimer

This is one person’s setup, not a study. I run a small company from home, so nobody watches me pace the kitchen and talk to my laptop. If you work in an open office, you’ll want a door. I’m a generalist, not an expert in any of this. Your mileage will vary.

The setup

Build the rig

Mine is a Mac. Windows works too: swap Controlly for a Windows equivalent (a quick search will find one).

1. Get the gear

  • An Xbox controller. Any Bluetooth model.
  • Wireless headphones with a decent mic. I use Bose 700s, but any solid pair works. The mic matters more than the brand: a bad mic means bad dictation, and bad dictation kills the whole thing.

2. Install the software

  • Claude desktop. Learn three shortcuts: ⌘1 for Chat, ⌘2 for Cowork, ⌘3 for Claude Code.
  • Controlly ($3.99 on the Mac App Store, 7-day free trial). It maps controller buttons to keyboard shortcuts. This is the piece that turns a game controller into a work tool.

3. Map the controller

Here is my mapping. Copy it, or tune your own. The whole goal is to run Claude without reaching for the keyboard or the mouse.

4. Work the loop

This is the part that makes it click. Turn on macOS dictation first (System Settings > Keyboard > Dictation), then point a button at it. I use the right trigger for ⌘D to start and stop dictation.

Then the loop is simple: walk, hold the trigger, talk. Your words land on the page at the speed you think them. Tap B to send. Right stick to scroll, left stick to move the cursor, A to click. You never sit down.

Why it works

Two things make this more than a gimmick. Voice is faster than typing: a 2017 Stanford study found dictation was three times faster than typing on a phone, with twenty percent fewer errors. And moving helps you think: a 2014 Stanford study found people came up with more ideas while walking than sitting, inside or out. The rig lets you do both at once, while Claude handles the expert part.

Where people get stuck

The standing-desk trap. Standing while typing is still typing. The keyboard is still the bottleneck. Don’t solve the wrong half of the problem: drop the keyboard and move.

Weak mic. Laptop mics are fine when you sit still. They’re rough from across the room. Use the headset mic, not the laptop.

Over-mapping. Start with four jobs: dictate, send, scroll, click. Add buttons once those are muscle memory. A controller with thirty jobs is a controller you won’t use.

Dictation left off. It’s off by default. Enable it before you map the trigger, or the button does nothing and you’ll think the rig is broken.

That’s the whole rig. Go walk the kitchen.
