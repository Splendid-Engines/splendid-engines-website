# Voice Profile: Founder LinkedIn voice
_Used for: LinkedIn posts, Email, Comments._
You are writing in my voice. Follow this for
HOW the writing reads, not WHAT it says.
## Style
- Tone: Friendly. Warm but clear.
- Reading level: grade 6.
- Concision: Concise. Short sentences.
## Greeting and address
- Open with "Hi {first name},".
- Use the person's first name.
## Phrasing
- Jargon: plain language.
## Hard rules (never break these)
- No em-dashes
- No emoji

Step 1 of 8 Basics

Name your voice

Your role and industry let us tailor the examples and the profile to your world. A quick sales DM and an engineering deep dive should not read the same. Everything here is optional.

Pick the formats and channels this voice is for. The AI adapts to whichever you are writing.

Reading level

Plain, easy-to-scan writing gets read and understood by more people. The lower the reading level, the wider the audience that will actually read it.

Tone

Tone is the first thing people feel. The same point can sound warm, sharp, or flat. Pick the one that fits you and your reader.

Style guide

Some publishers require a set style guide. Newsrooms and PR wires usually expect AP. Books and journals often want Chicago. If you write for one of these, match it. If not, leave it blank.

Optional. Tap one, or tap it again to clear.

Concision

Length changes how much gets read. Short, lean copy gets read and understood faster. Go longer only when the idea needs the room.

Not set
ConciseExpansive

We lean concise. Short sentences get read and understood faster.

“Omit needless words.”Strunk & White, The Elements of Style, rule 17

Greetings

The opening word sets the mood fast. "Hey" feels casual. "Dear" feels formal. Pick the ones that fit so the AI does not guess.

Phrasing

These small habits make writing sound like you. They set how formal you are and how easy you are to read.

Address people by first name, last name, or no preference?

When someone has a title like Dr. or Prof., use it with their last name?

Use industry jargon, or keep it plain?

Short forms like TBD and WFH, or spell everything out?

Write the way people talk, or use full sentences?

Hard rules

Hard rules are the lines the AI must never cross. Bans like em-dashes or emoji get followed every time.

Tap a rule to switch it on. A checkmark means it is on.

“Never use a metaphor, simile, or other figure of speech which you are used to seeing in print.”George Orwell, Politics and the English Language, 1946
